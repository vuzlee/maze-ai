AdaGrad & RMSProp

A step size per weight from the running squared gradient: AdaGrad sums it and stalls, RMSProp averages it and keeps moving.

01

AdaGrad

AdaGrad divides each weight's step by the root of the sum of all its past squared gradients — the first per-weight step size.

  • The sum never forgets, so every step is smaller than the last; on a long run it stalls before the minimum.
  • Still useful for sparse features (rare words, embeddings): a weight that rarely gets a gradient keeps a large step.
  • RMSProp, next, keeps the per-weight idea but swaps the sum for a decaying average.
02

RMSProp

Divide each weight's step by the root of its recent squared gradients: steep directions slow down, flat ones speed up.

  • AdaGrad came first and sums all past squares, so its steps only ever shrink; RMSProp replaces the sum with a decaying average.
  • The ratio of the two bars is the valley's elongation, undone per weight — no single learning rate can do that.
