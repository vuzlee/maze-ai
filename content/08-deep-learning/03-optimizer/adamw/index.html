AdamW

Weight decay taken out of the gradient: why L2 inside Adam barely shrinks big-gradient weights, and why AdamW is the default.

01

L2 inside Adam

Adding the L2 penalty λw to the gradient sends it through Adam's division, so weights with big gradients barely decay.

  • The penalty is meant to be the same pull toward 0 for every weight; dividing by √v̂ makes it weak exactly where gradients are large.
  • This is what torch.optim.Adam(weight_decay=…) does — the setting looks like weight decay but is L2 inside Adam.
  • The fix, next, takes the decay out of the gradient.
02

Decoupled weight decay

Apply weight decay to the weight directly, outside Adam's division, so every weight decays at the same rate.

g ← ∇L + λwL2 inside Adam: decay gets divided by √v̂
w ← w − η m̂√v̂ + ε − ηλwAdamW: decay applied as is
  • With plain SGD the two are identical; only an adaptive optimizer rescales the penalty. Why decay helps at all is in Dropout & regularization.
  • AdamW with λ ≈ 0.01–0.1, warmup and cosine decay is the standard recipe for LLMs; biases and norm weights are usually excluded from decay.
