Adam

Momentum and RMSProp in one update, with bias correction for the first steps — and why it needs so little tuning.

01

Adam update

Momentum's m for direction, RMSProp's v for size, and a bias correction because both start at zero.

m ← β1m + (1 − β1) gaverage gradient · v ← β2v + (1 − β2) g2average squared gradient
w ← w − η m̂√v̂ + εupdate , m̂ = m1 − β1t , v̂ = v1 − β2tbias correction
  • On step 1 the corrected ratio is just the sign of the gradient, so every weight moves by exactly η: Adam's step size is set by η, not by the gradient's scale.
  • Defaults β1 = 0.9, β2 = 0.999, ε = 10−8; it needs little tuning, which is why it is the default for transformers and most new models.
02

Adam vs SGD

Adam converges fast with little tuning; SGD + momentum is cheaper in memory and often generalises a little better.

  • Adam wins on transformers, LLMs, sparse or badly scaled gradients and short tuning budgets — the default for new models.
  • SGD + momentum wins on image CNNs (ResNet on ImageNet): with a tuned schedule it often reaches slightly higher test accuracy — the generalisation gap; AdamW narrows it.
  • Adam keeps two extra numbers per weight (m, v); at LLM scale that is why 8-bit optimizers and sharded optimizer state exist.
