Distributed systems · fundamentals

CAP, consistency & consensus

Copies of data on many machines must agree.

  • CAP: during a network split, pick consistency or availability
  • strong vs eventual consistency
  • consensus: a majority agrees on one leader
01

CAP theorem

Of Consistency, Availability and Partition tolerance, a distributed system can't have all three.

  • Network splits will happen, so P is not optional — the real choice is CP or AP.
02

The cases

What each choice looks like.

2.1Normal operation

No split: consistent and available.

2.2CP — refuse

During a split, return an error rather than old data.

Banking, inventory, leader election.

2.3AP — answer anyway

During a split, answer with what you have.

Social feeds, shopping carts, DNS.

2.4CA — one machine

No network between copies — but no tolerance either.

03

Consistency models

How soon a write is visible everywhere.

3.1Strong consistency

Every read sees the latest write.

Correct, but each write waits for the copies.

3.2Eventual consistency

Copies catch up after a delay.

Fast and highly available; the default in most NoSQL stores.

04

Agreement

How copies agree without a single boss.

4.1Quorum

Write to some, read from some — make them overlap.

With 3 copies: write to 2, read from 2.

4.2Leader election

A majority vote picks one leader.

  • Use an odd number of nodes: 3 or 5.
  • Raft and Paxos are the algorithms; etcd and ZooKeeper are the tools.
