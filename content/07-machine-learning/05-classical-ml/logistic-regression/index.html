Logistic regression

Take the straight line of linear regression, squash its score into a probability with a sigmoid, and train it with log loss instead of MSE.

01

Mental model

Every sample goes x → z → p: a linear score, squashed into a probability. A threshold you choose turns p into a label.

  • Running example all lesson: six customers A–F, x is age, the label is bought or not.
  • The model stops at p, and that p is well calibrated out of the box; the label step is a threshold you choose (section 06).
02

General formula

The score z is the linear part, the sigmoid turns it into a probability, and log loss charges for confidence in the wrong answer.

z = w·x + bscore, from −∞ to +∞ , p = 11 + e−zsigmoid σ(z): squashed into (0, 1)
Llog loss = − 1n Σi [ yi log pi + (1 − yi) log(1 − pi) ]y is 0 or 1, so only one of the two terms survives
  • L is also called binary cross-entropy; it is the negative log-likelihood of the labels.
  • A confident right answer costs almost 0; a confident wrong answer costs without limit.
03

Learning on a table

Training repeats two passes: forward fills p and the loss row by row, backward turns the errors into a slope and nudges w.

3.1Forward pass

Each row becomes z, then p, then its loss; the model's loss is the mean of the last column.

  • Customer E (52, did not buy) costs most: the model is sure older customers buy.
  • The boundary is always flat, so no w can fit E without hurting the others; a curved boundary needs squares or interactions you add yourself (Feature engineering).

3.2Gradient step

The slope is the mean of error × input, the same shape as in linear regression with ŷ replaced by p, not a coincidence but a property of the canonical link.

∂L∂w = 1n Σi (pi − yi) · xierror times input, averaged , w ← w − η ∂L∂wstep against the slope, learning rate η
  • There is no closed form like the normal equation; the loss is convex, so gradient descent reaches the single minimum.
  • Scale the features first, or a feature like age dominates the slope.
04

Why sigmoid and log loss

A straight line does not stop at 0 and 1, and MSE paired with a sigmoid kills the learning signal exactly on the worst mistakes.

4.1Sigmoid

A straight line does not stop at 0 and 1; the sigmoid does, for any input.

  • The sigmoid only rescales the score; the boundary z = 0 is still a straight line or flat plane.

4.2Log loss

With a sigmoid, MSE stops pushing exactly on the worst mistakes; log loss keeps pushing.

  • MSE with a sigmoid is also non-convex, so where you stop depends on where you start; log loss is convex, with a single minimum.
  • Regularize like any linear model with L1 or L2, as in Ridge, Lasso & Elastic Net; in scikit-learn C is 1/λ, so raising C means less regularization. Without it, perfectly separable classes push w to infinity and every p to 0 or 1.
05

Odds ratio

The linear part is the log-odds, so one more unit of x multiplies the odds by ew rather than adding to them.

log p1 − plog-odds = w·x + bexactly the linear part , ewodds ratio: odds multiply by this per unit of x
  • Add on log-odds, multiply on odds, no tidy rule on probability: the odds double, but p only rises from 30% to 46%, which is what to tell a non-specialist audience.
  • Readable coefficients and a stable fit on few samples make it the default baseline; when only accuracy on tabular data matters, gradient boosting usually wins.
06

Decision threshold

The model stops at a probability. The cut from p to a label is a business decision, not a model parameter.

  • 0.5 is nothing special; choose by the cost of a missed buyer versus a false alarm. With heavily imbalanced classes, also reweight the classes or move the threshold far from 0.5.
  • Measuring the trade-off: precision and recall, and ROC and PR curves over every threshold.
07

Softmax for many classes

With K classes, give each its own score and exponentiate then normalise; with two classes this is the sigmoid again.

  • Called multinomial logistic regression; the loss becomes cross-entropy over K classes.
  • The other option, one-vs-rest, trains K separate binary models.
