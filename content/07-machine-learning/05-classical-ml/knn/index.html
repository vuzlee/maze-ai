KNN — k nearest neighbours

KNN learns nothing: it keeps the training table and, for each new row, lets the k most similar rows decide.

01

Mental model

To predict a new row, find the k rows closest to it in the stored data and let them decide.

  • Nothing is fitted, so KNN is called a lazy learner or non-parametric model.
  • The boundary has no fixed shape: it follows the data, wiggles and all.
02

03

From table to prediction

Predicting is sorting by distance, keeping the top k rows and counting their votes.

  • New customer X: age 25, income 35M; both columns scaled before measuring.
  • The cost moves to prediction time: one full scan per new row (section 5.3).
04

Knobs

The model is exactly three choices: how many neighbours, how to combine them, and what "close" means.

4.1Number of neighbours k

Same sorted rows, different k, different answer: k sits right on the bias–variance axis.

  • k = 1 memorises noise (high variance, training error 0 — meaningless); k = n always predicts the majority (high bias).
  • Pick k by cross-validation; an odd k avoids ties with two classes; weights="distance" lets nearer rows count more.

4.2Vote or mean

Classification takes the most common label; regression takes the mean value.

  • For long-tailed targets use the median, the same story as MAE vs MSE in Linear regression.
  • In sklearn: KNeighborsClassifier votes, KNeighborsRegressor averages.

4.3Distance metric

"Close" is a choice: choosing the metric is choosing the model.

  • Euclidean for continuous columns of the same kind; Manhattan is less hurt by one huge difference; cosine for text and embeddings.
05

Where it breaks

Every weakness comes from the same place as its strength: everything rides on raw distance.

5.1Unscaled columns

The column with the widest range decides the distance alone unless you scale first.

  • Fit the scaler on the training set only, then apply it to new rows.
  • Trees compare one column at a time, so they do not care; every distance-based model does.

5.2Curse of dimensionality

As dimensions grow, every point is about equally far from every other.

  • Reduce dimensions first (PCA, feature selection) or switch to trees.
  • Learned embeddings still work in hundreds of dimensions because the data lies on a low-dimensional surface.

5.3Slow prediction

Training is free, so every prediction pays: O(n · d) per query, and the whole table stays in memory.

  • Good as a quick baseline or for similarity search; for many rows or columns prefer trees or boosting.
