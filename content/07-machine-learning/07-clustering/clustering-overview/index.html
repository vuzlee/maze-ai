Clustering overview

With no labels, a clustering method decides what "a group" means — a centre, a dense region, a chain of near points, or a bell curve — and that choice decides which shapes it can find.

01

Same points, four families

Run each family on the same points and the definition of a group becomes visible: only density and chains follow the moon.

  • Agglomerative clustering starts with every point alone and joins the two closest groups again and again; the record of joins is a dendrogram, cut at a height. "Closest" needs a linkage: single, complete, average or Ward. One tree gives every number of clusters, but pairwise distances cost O(n2) memory.
  • A Gaussian mixture model (GMM) is k-means with soft membership and tilted ellipses, fitted by EM; it still needs k and convex shapes.
  • Distance needs features on one scale — standardise first (Feature engineering). Scoring a clustering with no labels (silhouette) is in K-means.
