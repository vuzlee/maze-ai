Machine learning · Clustering

Clustering overview

With no labels, a clustering method decides what "a group" means — a centre, a dense region, a chain of near points, or a bell curve — and that choice decides which shapes it can find.

01

Mental model

Only distances are given; close points should share a group, and a few points belong to none.

  • The same 33 points run through every figure below: two round blobs, a moon, three outliers.
  • Distance needs features on one scale — standardise first (Feature engineering).
02

Families

Four definitions of a group; run each on the same points and see which shapes it catches.

2.1Centroid · k-means

A group is the points nearest one centre; move the centres to the means until nothing changes.

  • Fast and simple, but you choose k, every group comes out round, and outliers are forced in.
  • Full lesson: K-means.

2.2Density · DBSCAN

A group is a dense region, grown from core points through their eps-neighbours; the rest is noise.

  • No k, any shape, real noise — paid for with one eps that must suit every cluster.
  • Full lessons: DBSCAN, then HDBSCAN for clusters of different density.

2.3Hierarchical · agglomerative

Start with every point alone, join the two closest groups again and again, and cut the record of joins — the dendrogram — at a height.

  • "Closest groups" needs a linkage: single (nearest pair), complete (farthest pair), average (mean of all pairs) or Ward (smallest rise in within-cluster variance). Single follows chains like the moon; Ward makes round groups like k-means.
  • One tree gives every number of clusters at once, but all pairwise distances cost O(n2) memory — fine for thousands of points, not millions.

2.4Distribution · Gaussian mixture

A group is one bell curve; every point gets a probability for each, and EM reshapes the curves to fit.

  • A Gaussian mixture model (GMM) is k-means with soft membership and stretched, tilted ellipses instead of circles; fitted by EM (expectation–maximisation).
  • The price: still k given, still convex shapes, and many more parameters that can collapse onto a few points.
03

Silhouette

No answer key, so score each point by how close it is to its own group against the nearest other group.

s(i)from −1 to 1 = b(i) − a(i)max(a(i), b(i))a = mean distance inside · b = to the nearest other cluster
  • Near 1: well inside its group. Near 0: on a border. Below 0: probably in the wrong group.
  • Silhouette rewards round, separated groups — it ranks k-means above DBSCAN here although DBSCAN found the true shapes. Use it to compare settings of one method, not families.
  • Cluster numbers are arbitrary: two runs may call the same group 0 and 2.
04

Learning order

Three lessons, each born to fix where the one before breaks.

  • K-means → DBSCAN → HDBSCAN.
  • For many features, read PCA too: in high dimensions every distance looks alike.
