Core concepts overview

This group is the thread tying together every model lesson that follows. Five lessons, not five separate topics — they are five stages of one question: how do we know the model really learned instead of memorizing.

01

Mental model

Measuring accuracy on the very data the model trained on is meaningless: a model that memorizes always scores perfectly there. Fix the measurement on a real case and the number drops from 1.000 to 0.460 — below even a blind guess.

  • The five gates are the group's five lessons, in order: gate 01 asks what kind of problem this is and what ruler measures it; gates 02–03 are splitting data; gate 04 is bias–variance and how to rein it in; gate 05 is feature engineering.
  • The leak at gate 02 throws no error: 0.667 looks perfectly respectable, and only a different split exposes it. That is why the data-splitting lesson devotes a whole section to six sources of leakage.
  • On tabular data, fixing the inputs beats tuning the model: raising k from 1 to 61 gains 6.0 points, while scaling and dropping two irrelevant features gains 13.7 points — more than twice as much, and cheaper.
