AdaBoost

Many stumps in sequence, each round piling weight onto the rows predicted wrong. Three things to remember:

  • the weight of wrong rows goes up for the next round
  • a stump with fewer errors gets a larger α, a weightier vote
  • the final prediction is the sign of the sum of votes α × h
01

Mental model

AdaBoost is many stumps in sequence: each round raises the weight of the rows the previous stump got wrong, then the stumps vote with weight α.

  • A stump is a tree of exactly one level: one question, two answers.
  • On its own a stump is almost useless, and that is the condition for boosting to work.
  • There is one set of formulas, derived from exponential loss. It gives err, α and the new weights.
  • Running example throughout: 6 customers A–F, y = +1 if they bought, −1 if not.
  • Row E (age 52, did not buy) sits between two customers who bought, so every stump has a hard time getting E right.
  • How one tree learns: Decision tree. The generalized version: Gradient boosting.
02

AdaBoost gradually lowers the model's exponential loss. From that loss come three linked formulas: err, α and the new weights.

Order of computation, round one:

  • No penalty term like XGBoost. The objective here is just the loss.
  • α and the new weights are not tricks. They are the fastest way to lower L round by round: section 05.
  • scikit-learn writes α doubled (ln instead of ½ ln). Predictions and weights are exactly the same.
03

From table to one stump

The stump picks the column and threshold with the smallest weighted err. Rows predicted wrong get more weight for the next round.

  • err is the total weight of the wrong rows, not the number of wrong rows. Count rows and every round picks the same stump.
  • err must be below 0.5. At 0.5 or above, α is negative or zero, and the stump is useless.
  • The stump must be weak. A strong model gets nearly everything right in round one, err goes to 0, α diverges, and there is nothing left to pile onto.
  • The figure only scans the first 5 stumps. In practice it tries every column, every threshold, both sign directions.
04

Adding up many stumps

The final prediction is a weighted vote: each stump casts a ±1 vote times α, add them up, take the sign.

  • The err of each stump does not fall steadily (0.17, 0.20, 0.19). Each round the problem is made harder. What improves is the sum of the votes.
  • The only information flowing from one round to the next is the weights. Drop them and every stump is identical.
05

Why the formulas take that form

The next round's weights are exactly e−margin, normalized. So rows with a negative margin always get a lot of weight.

  • A positive margin is right, a negative one is wrong. L falls every round (0.75, 0.60, 0.47). The number of wrong rows does not have to fall.
  • The weights are the gradient of L at each row. AdaBoost is gradient boosting with exponential loss: Gradient boosting.
  • A mislabeled row keeps a negative margin forever, so its weight grows without bound. The model pours its effort into learning a broken row.
  • A truly mislabeled row gets weight in exactly this way. Train loss still falls; only test gets worse.
06

Pros and cons

Every strength and weakness comes from pouring effort into the rows that are currently wrong.

AspectGainLose
AccuracyCombines many weak stumps into a strong model, with few parametersOn tabular data it usually loses to gradient boosting
Label noiseWorks well on data with clean labelsWrong labels pull weight forever: its most serious weakness
Reading the modelEach round is visible: which rows get heavier, which stump has a large αMany stumps added together cannot be read rule by rule
LossClosed-form formula for αFixed to exponential loss, classification only
TrainFew parameters to tuneSequential, cannot run rounds in parallel
  • Choose AdaBoost to learn the principle of boosting, or when the labels are clean.
  • Choose gradient boosting for real problems: the loss can be swapped, so it handles noise better. See Gradient boosting.
  • Choose random forest when you suspect heavily dirty labels: it averages, rather than pouring effort into the wrong rows.
