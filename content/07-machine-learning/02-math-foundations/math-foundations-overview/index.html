01

How to read this map

Not a math course. Four lessons, and the first three form one chain from an assumption about the data to the final set of parameters.

  • Math does just two jobs here: write down the loss function, and find where it is smallest.
  • Every lesson serves one of those two jobs — none is a standalone subject.
  • The level needed is reading and explaining, not proving.
  • The chain ends at MLE & MAP at the end of Probability, so that is where it all comes together.

Where each piece comes back

The figure above says what each lesson does in the chain. This table says which lessons you will run into it again — the reason not to skip any.

LessonComes back in

CalculusLogistic regression · gradient boosting · all of deep learning

  • The right column is thickest for Probability and Calculus — these two come back in nearly every later lesson, so studying them well pays off most.
  • Statistics sits outside the chain but gets used daily: every reported number is a sample mean, and you must ask how much it fluctuates.
03

Learning order

The learning order is not the chain order: the chain follows logic, while this list follows what each lesson needs first.

1 · ProbabilityThe longest in the group and the base of the other three: random variables, expectation, Bayes, then MLE and MAP. Read top to bottom, each section needs the one before.
2 · Linear algebraIndependent of lesson 1, but needed before linear regression and PCA.
3 · CalculusNeeds lesson 2: the gradient of a multivariable function is a vector.
4 · StatisticsRuns the other way: from sample to population. Needs the law of large numbers and the CLT from lesson 1.
  • In a hurry, read the first three sections of Probability then go to Calculus — enough to start Core concepts and Linear regression.
  • Don't stop here to "master the math first" — these ideas only sink in when you meet them again inside a concrete model.
  • Statistics can be read any time after lesson 1, no need to finish the group first.
