Math foundations — the map

Not a math course. Four lessons, each with one job:

  • math in ML does two things: write down the loss and find where it is smallest
  • probability, linear algebra and calculus form one chain; statistics checks the result
  • reading and explaining is enough — no proofs
01

Mental model

Fit a line to points: probability says what “wrong” costs, calculus walks downhill to the cheapest line.

  • The loss is one number per choice of parameters; the curve on the right is that number for every slope.
  • Training = rolling down that curve. Every model on this shelf does the same, with more parameters.

The four lessons

Each lesson owns one step of the picture above.

2.1Probability

Assume the noise is a bell curve; minus its log is the squared error — that is where MSE comes from.

L=− log p(y | x)how surprised the model is∝(y − ŷ)2squared error, for Gaussian noise
  • Lesson: Probability — distributions, expectation, Bayes, MLE.
  • Comes back in Naive Bayes, log loss, Ridge and Lasso.

2.2Linear algebra

Data is a matrix, weights are a vector; one product predicts every row at once.

ŷone prediction per row=Xrows × featureswone weight per feature
  • Lesson: Linear algebra — vectors, dot product, matrices.
  • Comes back in every linear model, PCA, embeddings and attention.

2.3Calculus

The derivative is the slope of the loss; step against it, and stop where it is zero.

wnew weight←wold weight−ηstep size∂L∂wslope of the loss
  • Lesson: Calculus — derivative, gradient, chain rule.
  • Comes back in Logistic regression, Gradient boosting and all of deep learning.

2.4Statistics

A score measured on a sample wobbles; an interval says how far the truth could be.

x̄sample mean±2≈ 95%σ√nstandard error
  • Lesson: Statistics — confidence intervals, tests, A/B testing.
  • Comes back whenever two models are compared: is the gap real or noise?
